# Human style: primary-evidence ledger and falsifiable research design

Research cut-off: 2026-10-02 UTC. Literature synthesis only. No owner corpus was analyzed, no new model was fitted, and no paper result below is a result of our project. Primary full texts were inspected for every numbered entry unless explicitly marked otherwise. “Human style” is not treated as a single axis or a detector score.

## 1. Answer to the substantive question

Sentence rhythm, paragraph transitions, and hierarchical organization are central parts of the requested object of study. A lexical inventory, even with 100 entries, does not establish that object. Nor does adding sentence-length SD and a semantic-similarity graph complete it. The necessary distinction is between **what forms occur, how they are arranged, what communicative work they perform, and how those choices change with context**.

The defensible working object is a conditional distribution of linguistic choices at multiple scales: writer, audience, register, task, topic, evidence, and textual position. This is an operational research definition, not a claim to have discovered a universal law of human authorship.

Three separate questions must remain separate:

1. **Human linguistic organization:** Which conditional regularities occur in authentic writing, including cohesion, argument organization, and information packaging?
2. **Source discrimination:** Which of those regularities distinguish specified human corpora from specified model/prompt/decoding regimes?
3. **Writing quality:** Which changes improve comprehension, relevance, credibility, or expressive fit for readers?

A feature can predict authorship without measuring quality. An AI text can be judged better than a novice human text. A genuinely human text can be highly repetitive because its genre requires it. These possibilities are compatible, not anomalies to eliminate.

## 2. Evidence ledger

### E1. Sentence length and syntax have no universal human–AI direction

**Primary:** Zanotto & Aroyehun, EMNLP 2025, [Linguistic and Embedding-Based Profiling of Texts Generated by Humans and Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1163.pdf), §§3–5,8; Table 1.

**Design:** 467,985 English texts, eight domains, eleven LLMs; compares text/sentence length, morphology, dependency depth/length, lexical and semantic measures across decoding/repetition-control conditions. Uses Mann–Whitney tests, descriptive means/SEs, and a logistic classifier.

**Finding:** No consistent average-sentence-length ordering across generators/settings. Human dependencies are generally shorter/shallower than older models, but newer models may match or undershoot them. Sampling and repetition penalties alter the results.

**Limit:** These are mostly marginal/aggregated observables, not a test of long–short sentence sequencing, paragraph-boundary rhythm, or role-conditioned syntax. The paper explicitly leaves feature interactions unanalyzed. English-only coverage and corpus construction limit transfer. A nonsignificant U-test is not an equivalence test; the paper's “indistinguishable” interpretation should not be repeated as proof of equality.

**Status:** Supports studying syntax and conditional model effects; rejects a fixed instruction such as “human sentences must be longer/more complex.”

### E2. Hierarchical discourse carries complementary information

**Primary:** Kim et al., ACL 2024, [Threads of Subtlety](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.298.pdf), §§2–5,7; Tables 3–4.

**Design:** DMRST parses produce labelled RST relations, nucleus/satellite roles, and nested spans; recursive hypergraphs yield motif frequencies/depths. HC3, a subset of DeepFakeTextDetect, and 20 Gutenberg stories with GPT-4-1106-preview filling 1/3/5-paragraph gaps provide evaluations.

**Finding:** Longformer + motifs improves OOD F1 .74→.82; paraphrased OOD .60→.62. Motifs alone yield only .49 OOD F1. Story generations increasingly use Elaboration/Temporal motifs, while human passages more often use certain Joint motifs.

**Controls/limits:** Includes unseen-domain/model and paraphrase tests, but depends on automatic parses and mostly unpaired benchmark texts. Unconstrained 8K-token stories were generated but excluded because of parser input limits. No universal hierarchy direction follows. Parser accuracy, graph size, domain composition, and motif selection must be audited before causal interpretation.

**Status:** Direct evidence for preserving rhetorical labels and hierarchy; not evidence that an unlabeled semantic graph or arbitrary tree-depth scalar measures human intentional organization.

### E3. Transition-word counts are not transition quality

**Primary:** Herbold et al., Scientific Reports 2023, [A large-scale comparison of human-written versus ChatGPT-generated essays](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-45644-9), Methods/Results; Tables 2–4.

**Design:** 90 topics, one non-native student essay and one essay from each of ChatGPT-3.5 and GPT-4 per topic: 270 essays, 658 ratings from 111 teachers. Seven linguistic measures compared with ratings; Holm–Bonferroni correction across 42 tests, bootstrap mean CIs, Cohen's d.

**Finding:** GPT-4 used fewer discourse markers yet received higher logical-composition/text-linking ratings. Marker count correlated weakly negatively with logic ratings (r=−.14). Syntactic depth and clause measures correlated only weakly positively with complexity ratings (r=.16/.19). Lexical-diversity ordering changed between GPT-3.5 and GPT-4.

**Limit:** The human comparison is a specific learner-writing population, not expert writing or all humans. The marker list is a surface proxy, not annotation of explicit/implicit relation correctness. Observational pooled correlations do not show that deleting markers improves coherence.

**Status:** Strong construct-validity warning: “however/therefore” frequency cannot stand in for whether a contrast/inference is licensed.

### E4. Local cohesion is about entity transitions, not just repeated words

**Primary:** Barzilay & Lapata, Computational Linguistics 2008, [Modeling Local Coherence: An Entity-Based Approach](https://aclanthology.org/J08-1001.pdf), §§3–4; Tables 4–5.

**Design:** Entity grids encode sentence-by-sentence grammatical roles and absence of discourse entities, with syntax/coreference/salience ablations. In each of earthquake news and aviation-accident reports, 100 training documents generate up to 20 sentence permutations each; separate test documents assess original-vs-permuted ordering.

**Finding:** Full-model pair-ranking accuracy is 87.2%/90.4%; removing coreference hurts earthquake texts substantially, while syntax contributes more in repetitive accident reports. Effects differ by domain.

**Limit:** This establishes local ordering sensitivity on a synthetic-coherence task, not a human–LLM distinction or complete global coherence. Original ordering is presumed preferable; arbitrary permutations are often easy negatives. Entity tracking cannot establish evidence sufficiency or causal validity.

**Status:** A validated relational construct for sentence and paragraph continuity; use realistic near-miss reorderings and human coherence judgments in extension.

### E5. Argument structure is independently annotatable

**Primary:** Stab & Gurevych, COLING 2014, [Annotating Argument Components and Relations in Persuasive Essays](https://aclanthology.org/C14-1142.pdf).

**Design:** Three annotators annotate 90 persuasive essays with argument components and support/attack relations. Reported agreement: unitized α=.72 for components; α=.81 for relations.

**Finding:** Claims, premises, and their directed relations are operationally distinguishable with explicit annotation guidelines and meaningful, imperfect agreement.

**Limit:** Reliability of this annotation scheme is not proof of argument validity, audience persuasiveness, or a human–AI effect. Persuasive-essay ontology does not automatically transfer to narrative, explanation, research methods, or Chinese discourse. Components may span less or more than one sentence.

**Status:** Supports a separate claim/evidence/rebuttal representation. RST elaboration is not equivalent to evidential support; topic similarity is neither.

### E6. Context-dependent information packaging has human evidence

**Primary:** Jaeger, Cognitive Psychology 2010, [Redundancy and reduction: Speakers manage syntactic information density](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2896231/), §§2.1–2.5.

**Design:** 6,716 optional-complementizer cases from 346 Switchboard speakers; multilevel logistic analysis. Controls include accessibility, ambiguity, dependency lengths, fluency, grammaticalization, and syntactic persistence. Information at clause onset is estimated from verb-conditioned complement-clause probability.

**Finding:** Higher estimated onset information predicts more overt “that” (β=.47, z=16.9, p<.0001); contribution is modest relative to total variability (partial Nagelkerke R²=.05).

**Limit:** Observational spontaneous English speech, a specific alternation, and a restricted probability proxy. This is not an AI comparison, a written-paragraph result, or evidence of conscious intention. It does not predict constant information in every word or random variation everywhere.

**Status:** Positive evidence that form depends on context and processing demands. The relevant observable is the choice conditioned on its opportunity/context, not global frequency alone.

### E7. A tempting global information principle does not simply replicate

**Primary:** Verma, Tomlin & Klein, Findings EMNLP 2023, [Revisiting Entropy Rate Constancy in Text](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.1039.pdf), §§3–6.

**Design:** Penn Treebank replication; NYT and post-2018 CC News each split 100K/10K/10K; Arabic Al-Ittihad 8,551/1,000/2,000. Trigrams versus GPT-2 sizes 124M–1.5B, fine-tuned/unadapted models, word probabilities aggregated from subwords.

**Finding:** Trigram surprisal growth replicates; long-context models instead show sharp initial decreases and corpus-dependent subsequent trends. This does not supply clear evidence for document-level entropy constancy.

**Limit:** Scorer/model/domain adaptation and document length alter the trajectory. Estimated surprisal is not the writer's or reader's exact information state. This result does not refute every local uniform-information-density claim.

**Status:** Supports measuring conditional trajectories while testing their scorer dependence; rejects treating a flat or maximally bursty trajectory as a universal target.

### E8. The OUP function-word result concerns population dispersion

**Primary:** Chen et al., Digital Scholarship in the Humanities 2026, [Stylometric detection of AI-generated texts](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/advance-article/doi/10.1093/llc/fqag064/8714041), §§3,5–6.

**Design:** 4,346 essays total, **2,173 pairs**, covering 110 topics. Aeon essays are paired by title with GPT-4o-mini outputs from one fixed 1,000-word generation instruction. Vectors contain 70 selected word proportions plus the remaining-token proportion.

**Finding:** MDS displays a more concentrated model cluster. Classifier performance changes with length, topic training, and treatment of the human class.

**Limit:** Approximately 1,500 actual authors are replaced by one distinct label per essay. This does not establish stable repeated-author signatures. Title matching does not match discourse purpose, evidence, editorial process, or exact length. The visualization and classifier improvement do not isolate covariance from mean/length/source differences, nor measure within-document burstiness. Exact label/training treatment needs code audit.

**Status:** A useful corpus-specific baseline, not a definition of humanity. The 71 proportions sum to one, so their covariance is constrained and singular; naïve independent-feature interpretation is invalid.

### E9. The 463,382-comment Weibo study is real, but requires design caution

**Primary:** Li & Zhang, CCL 2025, [Linguistic Differences between AI and Human Comments in Weibo](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ccl-1.64.pdf), §§2–3.

**Design:** 2024 comments collected around officially disclosed bots; 417,361 human-labelled plus 46,021 bot comments. A subset uses 34 lexical/pragmatic/readability/symbol/sentiment features and an 80:20 sample split.

**Finding:** Bot comments are generally longer, more affectionate, and more conventionally punctuated/error-free. SFSC F1=.918 versus .907 without attention; triggered-bot false negatives 5.26%, voluntary 11.94%. Personalized blogger bots are harder to detect.

**Limit:** The “balanced” subset reports 18,337+18,337 AI versus 33,674 human comments, an arithmetic inconsistency. No demonstrated account/thread-disjoint or forward-time generalization follows from the stated sample split. Many features are counts; length is a plausible driver. Bot product/persona/function and human source are confounded; undisclosed AI use may contaminate human labels. Attention gain does not identify causal linguistic interactions.

**Status:** Chinese-social-media evidence, not evidence for long-form paragraph hierarchy or all Chinese human writing.

### E10. Original intrinsic-dimension evidence and its stated boundary

**Primary:** Tulchinskii et al., NeurIPS 2023, [Intrinsic Dimension Estimation for Robust Detection of AI-Generated Texts](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7baa48bc166aa2013d78cbdc15010530-Paper-Conference.pdf), §§4–6, Appendix B. [Author code](https://github.com/ArGintum/GPTID).

**Design:** Per-text contextual-token clouds from RoBERTa-base/XLM-R; zero-dimensional persistence via MST length scaling, α=1. Appendix uses eight subset sizes starting at 40, seven subsamples per size, median length, d=1/(1−slope). English transfer compares Wikipedia, Reddit, StackExchange and GPT-2/OPT/GPT-3.5; ten-language tests use ChatGPT.

**Finding:** Lower model ID in tested fluent-output regimes; multilingual PHD AUC ranges .709–.833. Authors explicitly warn that high-temperature/broken generations may exceed human ID and short samples are unstable.

**Limit:** A representational geometric statistic, not a direct syntactic/discourse construct. Layer/metric/restart/truncation details require code matching. Appendix subset formula and endpoint prose appear inconsistent. Six truncated examples establish pipeline execution only, not replication or learned integration.

**Status:** Candidate auxiliary observable, with original replication kept separate from controlled extensions.

### E11. Newer ID evidence challenges domain invariance

**Primary:** Pedashenko et al., EACL 2026, [Unveiling Intrinsic Dimension of Texts: from Academic Abstract to Creative Story](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.370.pdf), §§4–5,8.

**Design:** Retains 172,750 of 261,758 COLING development texts; excludes <150 Gemma tokens or unstable/out-of-[2,18] PHD under any of Gemma-2-2B, Qwen-2.5-1.5B, RoBERTa-base. Includes linguistic correlations, encoder comparisons, SAE steering and rewriting experiments.

**Finding:** Contrary to the earlier invariance claim, scientific/technical sources average roughly 7.5–8, factual/news 8–9, creative/opinion 9–10.5. PHD associates positively with lexical diversity and negatively with overlap/repetition; syntactic metrics are weaker. Entropy association largely disappears with length adjustment.

**Limit:** Filtering on estimator values can change observed distributions. Encoder/estimator choices remain substantive; steering effects are interventions on a specific model, not proof of human cognitive mechanism. Negative-direction rewrite effects are inconsistent.

**Status:** Strong reason to control genre/repetition/length before interpreting geometry. PHD is not “how human” a document is.

### E12. Editing and generation are distinct empirical conditions

**Primary, preprint:** Shan, Lee & Hao, August 2026, [AI Writers Have a Consistent Stylometric Footprint, but AI Editors Do Not](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27855v1), §§3–6, Appendices B–D.

**Design:** Approximately 45K human/generated texts, eight open-weight LLMs, five English domains; 273,420 analyzed edits from three models and 31 editing instructions. Fourteen features, linear classifiers, length controls, generator/domain analyses.

**Finding:** Generated texts have higher empirical word-frequency entropy/diversity in these regimes; edits instead reduce entropy with only a small diversity increase. Most features depend strongly on generator/domain.

**Limit:** “Entropy” here is bag-of-word-frequency entropy, not conditional LM surprisal. “Burstiness” is dispersion of word-type counts, not sentence-length sequencing. The study is a recent preprint; repeated variants, feature-selection leakage, and exact held-out-unit treatment warrant independent code audit.

**Status:** Useful counterexample to universal lexical slogans and a required generation/editing distinction, not the proposed project's proof.

### E13. Human author identification is confounded by topic unless explicitly tested

**Primary:** Stamatatos, EACL 2017, [Authorship Attribution Using Text Distortion](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1107.pdf), §5; Tables 4–5.

**Design:** Masks less frequent/topic-bearing words while preserving frequent forms, then compares attribution in ordinary, cross-topic, and cross-genre settings. Guardian cross-topic evaluation uses every one-topic-train/one-topic-validation/two-topic-test assignment; cross-genre trains on book reviews and evaluates opinion writing.

**Finding:** Distortion raises average character-ngram cross-topic accuracy 66.2→75.1%; cross-genre 39.4→58.9%, with acknowledged remaining difficulty.

**Limit:** Attribution concerns a fixed set of known authors, not all human writers. Masking reduces topical cues but does not mathematically eliminate them or prove a context-free author essence.

**Status:** Stable author signature requires repeated documents and held-out tasks/topics/time, not a human population cloud or one essay per author.

### E14. Sentence rhythm is measurable; structural interpretation needs negative controls

**Primary:** Vieira et al., 2018, [Robustness of sentence length measures in written texts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.01460), §§2–3: 501 English-language fiction books, six word/character/lemmatized/content-word length definitions, Pearson/distribution/DFA comparisons. Similarity across definitions supports measurement robustness for that corpus, not AI separation.

**Primary, preprint counterexample:** Zeng et al., 2025, [Fractal Illusions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19782): randomized punctuation, keystroke sequences, and mixed-source sentences can also produce reported long-range/fractal signatures. Narrow examples and estimator choices limit generalization; this is a warning against equating a Hurst exponent with intentional literary structure.

**Status:** Use sentence-length time series, but first validate segmentation, stationarity, document-size requirements, and shuffled/synthetic controls. A variance or fractal exponent alone cannot tell whether a short sentence resolves an argument, starts a contrast, or merely occurs by chance.

## 3. What has and has not been established

| Hypothesis | Current judgment | Discriminating test still required |
|---|---|---|
| Human and selected AI corpora differ in feature means | Repeatedly supported in bounded settings; directions vary | Paired task/source/length estimates with model × genre interactions and held-out replication |
| Humans are universally more variable | Unsupported as a universal; several population contrasts support narrower versions | Separate between-author, within-author, between-task, between-model/prompt, and within-document variance |
| Full covariance matters beyond means | Plausible; not established by a scatterplot or classifier accuracy | Mean/marginal-controlled residual-covariance comparison with equal training budget and held-out sources |
| Conditional sequence dynamics matter beyond unordered distributions | Linguistically motivated and partly supported by coherence/order studies | Histogram-preserving order manipulations plus role/boundary-conditioned predictive comparisons |
| Rhetorical hierarchy adds information beyond surface forms | Supported by E2 within its benchmarks | Gold-parse audit, length/EDU controls, label-vs-topology ablations, modern held-out generators |
| More connectives imply better coherence | Contradicted as a monotonic proxy | Relation correctness and reader comprehension, not keyword frequency |
| Higher ID means more human or better writing | Not supported; regime/domain counterexamples | Controlled geometry extension and independent quality ratings |
| An individual has a stable context-free signature | Not established; strong context dependence | Repeated-author cross-topic/time evaluation and variance decomposition |
| Cross-level dependencies are the defining human difference | **Untested project hypothesis** | Human-only construct validation, then matched human–AI comparison, then incremental predictive validity |

The most important mathematical distinctions:

- **Population dispersion:** variation among document summaries. A mixture of many writers/tasks can be broad even if each writer is internally consistent.
- **Within-document dispersion:** variability among a document's sentences/paragraphs. It is not the same estimand.
- **Sequence dynamics:** which values follow which others, conditional on position and function. Two texts can have identical length distributions but different rhythms.
- **Covariance:** co-variation across measured quantities. It does not by itself retain order, and may arise from a common cause such as length.
- **Hierarchy:** typed relations between spans at different levels. A text's tree may preserve nested argument scope while its feature covariance does not.
- **Author stability:** repeatability across an individual's independent documents after context adjustment. It cannot be estimated from a singleton.

## 4. Construct-driven observables and their validity tests

These are measurement families tied to hypotheses, not a request to enlarge an arbitrary feature count. No direction is declared “human” in advance unless the hypothesis is explicitly restricted to a source's conditions.

### A. Sentence rhythm and syntactic packaging

Retain the ordered sentence sequence, clause segmentation, paragraph membership, and rhetorical role. Measure the sentence-length distribution separately from lagged dependence, short/long runs, position effects, and boundary-associated changes. Keep dependency distance/depth, coordination/subordination, fragments, and information-bearing main-clause placement distinct from length.

Validation: manually audit sentence boundaries; compare tokens/characters where appropriate; control quoted dialogue, headings, lists, punctuation conventions, and sentence count. Permute complete sentences to preserve all sentence lengths; permute only within paragraphs to preserve paragraph composition; then permute only within annotated roles. These progressively distinguish generic order, paragraph structure, and role-aligned rhythm. Include length-matched grammatical alternatives so a syntactic result is not merely a length result.

### B. Local and paragraph cohesion

Track entity introduction, maintenance, reintroduction, grammatical-role changes, antecedent distance, and unresolved reference. Record where adjacent paragraphs share a referent versus where a new subtopic is introduced and linked back. Lexical/embedding overlap is a secondary cue.

Validation: annotate actual explicit and implicit discourse relations, their argument spans, and whether each relation is licensed by the content. Test referent substitutions, near-miss paragraph swaps, incorrect connective substitutions, and implicit-relation-preserving paraphrases. A valid cohesion measure should respond to broken reference while not rewarding redundant repetition indiscriminately.

### C. Discourse and argument hierarchy

Preserve typed span-to-span relations, nucleus/satellite status, parent/child nesting, attachment distance, and normalized depth/branching. In argumentative prose separately represent central claim, subclaim, evidence, warrant when expressed, objection, rebuttal, and conclusion; annotate support/attack direction and scope. A topic cluster is not an argument tree.

Validation: independently double-annotate a stratified subset; report span and relation agreement, not just a single score. Compare gold and automatic parses by human/model/genre. Ablate relation labels, nuclearity, hierarchy, and EDU text independently. Use trees matched for number of EDUs/relations. Check whether a model identifies a detached premise, scope error, or unsupported conclusion when lexical content and length remain nearly constant.

### D. Information progression and stance

Measure introduction/reuse of propositions and entities, evidence-to-claim placement, definitions before use, scope of qualifications, and whether certainty is attached to the claim or only to its evidence. Contextual surprisal can help characterize predictability but is scorer-dependent. Distinguish author stance from quoted/attributed stance and literal uncertainty from polite formulae.

Validation: score the same content under local, paragraph, and full-prefix context; test multiple frozen scorers and compare their rankings. Human annotators judge what is new, presupposed, supported, or contradicted. Do not infer conscious intent from text statistics; a functional relation can be observed without attributing a mental state.

### E. Cross-level dependence: the central unresolved hypothesis

Examples of legitimate hypotheses are whether sentence shortening occurs preferentially at conclusions or contrasts; whether a paragraph's introductory sentence establishes referents used by its evidence; whether syntactic compression changes when information is already given; and whether evidence strength predicts hedge scope. These couple form to function and position.

For each hypothesis, identify its unit, opportunities, conditioning variables, and null. Do not call a global correlation “conditional dynamics.” Analyze unconditional organization and role-conditioned realization separately: rhetorical role may be part of the phenomenon, not merely a nuisance to remove.

## 5. Exact replication and comparison protocol

1. **Freeze the target and provenance.** Human originals, AI full generation, AI editing of humans, and human editing of AI are separate cells. Preserve author/account, source, topic/prompt, time, genre/register, language, model/version, decoding settings, and editing provenance. Deduplicate before splitting.

2. **Reproduce papers before improving them.** E1 uses its exact corpus and extractors; E2 uses its exact parser/graph conversion/motif selection and splits; E8 reproduces title pairing/labels/word denominator and length conditions; E9 resolves subset arithmetic and split unit; E10 reproduces original embedding/scaling/restart choices. Changes are named controlled extensions, not quiet “replication fixes.” Obtain code/data where accessible; otherwise record unreproduced components explicitly.

3. **Define independent units.** Keep all derivatives of a source text, prompt, thread, and author in a single fold where the relevant inference requires it. For Weibo run separate account-disjoint, thread-disjoint, and chronological tests. For author signatures, within-author documents are required; for new-author generalization, leave whole authors out. A hundred generated variants do not become a hundred independent human sources.

4. **Separate length from style.** Report raw ecological comparisons and matched/reweighted token, sentence, paragraph, and EDU-count comparisons. Use several prespecified lengths and positions, including interior passages; first-200-word truncation is a positional condition, not a general short-text sample. Rates/count offsets and compositional treatment must be explicit. Never normalize away the outcome one is trying to study without reporting both estimands.

5. **Establish measurement validity before a detector.** Audit segmentation, parsers, coreference and discourse annotation on a blinded stratified sample. Report disagreement and differential error. Use targeted perturbations to verify that each measure responds to its intended construct and not merely lexical overlap or total length. Do not let an LLM judge its own outputs without independent human calibration.

6. **Compare a nested evidence ladder.** (a) nuisance-only model; (b) marginal means/rates; (c) within-document distributions; (d) residual covariance/interactions; (e) ordered local dynamics; (f) typed hierarchy; (g) cross-level conditional models. Keep tuning budget/training data controlled, report incremental held-out performance and conditional effect estimates. Multivariate models can exploit means as well as covariance, so center/match marginals and use appropriate permutation nulls to test the specific claim.

7. **Use structure-preserving nulls.** Sentence shuffling preserves sentence-length marginals; within-paragraph shuffling preserves paragraph composition; order-preserving lexical substitutions test reliance on vocabulary; label permutation on a fixed tree tests rhetorical labels; topology permutation with relation inventory preserved tests organization. For contextual-embedding PHD, distinguish permuting an already-computed point cloud (exactly invariant) from re-encoding shuffled text (changes contextual embeddings). The latter does not prove PHD explicitly represents hierarchy.

8. **Estimate uncertainty at the correct level.** Cluster-bootstrap paired source/prompt/author units. Report paired effect sizes and CIs, domain/model heterogeneity, multiplicity control, and estimator noise. Use equivalence margins to claim practical similarity. Do not infer covariance differences from MDS screenshots, or causality from attention weights/SHAP/classifier importances.

9. **Test transfer and quality independently.** Freeze features/tuning before evaluating held-out genres, sources, authors, prompt families and generators. Also obtain blinded human judgments of relation validity, comprehensibility, task fit and style, with facts preserved. Detection accuracy and perceived naturalness are different endpoints.

10. **Stop at the supported conclusion.** A successful paper reproduction supports that paper's conditions. A robust conditional finding across held-out settings supports a broader but still bounded claim. Only then can an intervention be evaluated, with factual/semantic preservation and reader benefit as requirements. Current literature does not justify a universal “humanize” transformation.

## 6. What is deliberately not claimed

- No evidence here proves that all humans favor maximal variance, shorter sentences, fewer transitions, deeper trees, or larger ID
- No covariance or cross-level dynamic model has yet been validated by our project
- No source inspected supplies a universal feature-weight vector for human style
- The 1996 Joyce Cary serial-correlation paper was located but its full text was not accessible through the attempted route; its abstract-level method was not promoted into a verified effect
- The recent Chinese academic-abstract move studies were located, but inaccessible full designs were not used as strong quantitative evidence
- Current evidence warrants analysis at sentence, paragraph, discourse, and cross-level scales; it does not warrant skipping those scales in favor of another lexical checklist or rewrite
